Abstract
Online tensor decompositions are powerful and proven techniques that address
the challenges in processing high-dimensional tensor data, such as traffic flow and
weather systems. The main aim of this work is to propose a more robust online
tensor train decomposition (OTTD) framework, which represents a spatial-temporal
continuous function using INRs continuity and easy adaptability for online data
with the added compact and low representation of the Tensor-Train decomposition.
The INRs allow for the natural characterization of continually expanding streaming
data by simply adding new coordinates into the network. Particularly, our method
transforms the classical online tensor decomposition algorithm into a more dynamic
continual learning paradigm of updating the INR weights to fit the new data without
forgetting the previous tensor knowledge in a compact manner. To this end, we
introduce a long-tail memory replay method that adapts to the local continuity
property of INR.
1 Introduction
Online tensor decomposition ( 2) is one of the foremost methods to address the streaming data
completion problem by exploiting the potential compact structures underlying streaming data within
an online optimization framework. However, decomposing tensor streams would lead to high
computational costs owing to the significant growth in their volume over time. Also, dynamically
capturing the internal latent properties of tensor streams, such as spatial-temporal continuity, poses
difficult challenges. Novel research (1) (OFTD) created a new online learning paradigm that leverages
INR’s in order to learn a more dynamic and easy to expand model then the original decomposition
paramteres.
In this work, we propose an improvement over OFTD with a better, more robust model for streaming
data completion. OTTD represents the spatial-temporal streaming data as a continuous function
using TT tensor decomposition parameterized by implicit neural representations (INRs) ( 34; 35),
which map an arbitrary spatial-temporal coordinate to the corresponding tensor value through deep
neural networks. Instead of optimizing the factor matrices of tensor decomposition, OTTD optimizes
the learnable weights of factor INRs during online optimization, which could better explore the
complex dynamic structure and global spatial-temporal correlations of streaming data through a deep
functional tensor representation. OTTD allows for natural characterizations of continually expanded
streaming data by simply adding new coordinates into the INR network during online optimization.
Although the tensor stream is continually growing, we maintain a constant number of optimization
parameters by keeping the network structure unchanged.
Nevertheless, the incorporation of deep neural networks leads to the possible issue of forgetting the
learned historical knowledge when fitting new data. Hence, to enable effective continual learning
for processing streaming tensors using OTTD, we make a basic attempt by employing the memory

2 Related Work
2.1 Online Tensor Decomposition
Online analysis algorithms for streaming tensors have been widely developed in recent years (19; 20;
21). The CP decomposition is one of the mostly considered methods for streaming tensor completion.
One issue with a CP decomposition is the sensitivity for the tensor rank ( 18) and finding the rank
of a tensor is an NP-hard problem. These online tensor methods explicitly optimize factor matrices
of the tensor decomposition using discrete online optimization rules. Different from these methods,
the OFTD (1) employs INRs and CP decomposition to model streaming data as a spatial-temporal
continuous function, which allows for natural characterization of continually expanded data by adding
new coordinates into INRs during online optimization. Our proposed OTTD uses that method with a
more robust tensor train model that should suffer less from the tensor rank.
2.2 Implicit Neural Representation
In recent years, INR has attracted widespread attention for their ability to continuously and implicitly
represent signals through neural networks Sitzmann et al. (34), Mildenhall et al. (35). Compared to
traditional discrete representations, INR offers advantages such as memory efficiency, continuous
trajectory modeling, and analytical computations of higher-order derivatives. Typical applications of
INR include 3D shape and scene reconstruction Atzmon and Lipman ( 36), Jiang et al. ( 37), view
synthesis Mildenhall et al. ( 38), solving differential equations Chen et al. ( 39), and images/video
processing Chen et al. (40), Skorokhodov et al. (41).
For instance, the well-known NeRF Mildenhall et al. (35) represents a scene as a continuous 5D func-
tion and optimizes neural radiance fields to synthesize novel views. Sitzmann et al. (34) introduced
the SIREN, which leverages periodic activation functions to achieve high-fidelity representation of
natural signals using INR. Chen et al. (40) proposed the local implicit image function, which encodes
images as continuous functions and enables arbitrary-resolution image reconstruction. Currently,
INR has become an increasingly popular research direction in mainstream AI fields Zhao et al. (42),
Shi et al. ( 43), Li et al. ( 44). To our knowledge, the proposed method should be the first work to
incorporate INR into the online tensor decomposition framework.

3.4 Theoretical Analysis
We interpret two insights of our method, i.e., the spatial-temporal continuity of OTTD brought from
INR, and the regret bound of the OTTD model that reveals its forgetting behavior, which motivates
us to design memory replay to alleviate forgetting. First, we show that our OTTD method preserves
the tensor spatial-temporal smoothness from the Lipschitz smooth perspective. Lemma 1 shows that
the TTD model preserves the spatial-temporal continuity of the estimated tensor Xt (i.e., elements
that are closer to each other are more likely to share similar structures).
Based on Lemma 1, we present a regret bound of OTTD regarding the loss of historical information
when fitting new data streams, which reveals the forgetting behavior of INR.
Theorem 1 shows that the regret bound is proportional to the positional distance (I t
n + 1−i n)
between the considered point (i1, i2, i3) and the new data stream position I t
n + 1. This indicates

5 Conclusion
We have proposed a novel streaming data completion method, OFTD, which utilizes Tensor-Train
(TT) decomposition parameterized by INRs to model tensor streams. By transforming classical online
tensor tracking into a continuous functional learning paradigm coupled with long-tail memory replay,
the model successfully adapts to new streaming data while mitigating catastrophic forgetting. Future
work will explore more advanced, buffer-free continual learning methods such as gradient projection
to further optimize the learning of INRs for infinite tensor streams.
